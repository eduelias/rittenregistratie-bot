Dutch trip log (rittenregistratie) requirements: what your mileage log must contain

Updated 2 November 2026 · sources: belastingdienst.nl

This page is general information, not tax advice. It is our summary of what the Belastingdienst (the Dutch Tax Administration) said on its website on 2 November 2026. Every rule links to its source so you can check it yourself. Most sources are only available in Dutch; where the Belastingdienst has an English page on the same rule, we link that as well. If you are unsure about your own situation, ask the Belastingdienst or your tax adviser.

Drive a company car or a lease car in the Netherlands and you will soon meet the word bijtelling: the taxable benefit for being able to use a business car privately. As an employee, your employer adds it to your taxable salary; as a business owner, you set it against your car costs. You avoid it by showing that you drive the car privately no more than 500 km a year, and the usual way to show that is a trip log, or rittenregistratie. This page explains what the Belastingdienst expects in one, with the Dutch terms you will see on forms and at your employer.

In short

  • A trip log (rittenregistratie) shows that you drive a company car no more than 500 km privately per calendar year. Then you do not pay bijtelling.
  • You record the car's details once and then, for every trip: date, start and end odometer reading, departure and arrival address, and whether it was private or business. You only add the route and private detour kilometres when they apply.
  • Going there and coming back are two trips, each on its own line.
  • Commuting between home and work counts as business.

When do you need a trip log?

That depends on who provides the car and what kind of car it is.

Your situationWhat applies
You run your own business (for example as a zzp'er, a self-employed person without staff, or an eenmanszaak, a sole proprietorship) and the car belongs to the business You set a bijtelling against your car costs, unless you can show that you drove no more than 500 km privately in the year. A trip log is the usual proof. As a business owner you cannot apply for a Verklaring geen privégebruik auto (the declaration of no private use of a car). (privégebruik auto, Dutch, rittenregistratie, Dutch)
You are an employee, or a dga (directeur-grootaandeelhouder, the director and major shareholder of your own bv), and drive a car provided by your employer You apply for a Verklaring geen privégebruik auto yourself. You must then be able to show that you drive no more than 500 km privately a year, for example with a trip log that is sluitend (complete and consistent, see below). (applying for the declaration, Dutch; English page for employers on the 500 km rule)
You have a van (bestelauto) with a Verklaring uitsluitend zakelijk gebruik bestelauto (declaration of business-only use of a van) You do not need to keep a trip log, but you may then not use the van privately at all. (uitsluitend zakelijk gebruik bestelauto, Dutch)
You run your own business and use your own, private car for business trips No bijtelling. In 2026 you may deduct € 0.25 per business kilometre (in 2024 and 2025 it was € 0.23). The Belastingdienst does not mention a trip log for this, but do keep track of your business kilometres so you can back up the deduction. (zakelijk gebruik privévervoermiddel, Dutch)

What must the trip log contain?

Car details

Once per log: the car's make (merk), model (type), registration number (kenteken) and the period in which you used the car. (source, Dutch)

If you drive from your work address to an appointment and then back again, the Belastingdienst counts that as 2 trips. So each trip gets its own line.

Extra for employees and dga's

The Handboek Loonheffingen (the payroll taxes manual the Belastingdienst writes for employers, only published in Dutch) asks a little more for a company car provided by an employer. You also record:

  • the start date and, if it is known, the end date of the period the car is made available to you;
  • a trip number per day (ritnummer);
  • if more than one person drives the car: who drove the trip.

You then only need to fill in the visiting address (bezoekadres) for business trips and mixed trips. (Handboek Loonheffingen 2026, paragraph 23.3.18, Dutch)

Simplified for vans and driving instructors

If you run a business and make many trips a day in a van, you may, under conditions, keep a simplified trip log. Driving instructors only record the start and end odometer reading for each working day. (source, Dutch)

What counts as private?

  • Commuting between home and work is business, both for business owners and for employees. (business owner, Dutch, employee, Dutch; English page for employers)
  • For employees: every kilometre you do not drive for your work is private. (source, Dutch)
  • If you make a detour for something private during a business trip, such as shopping, you record the extra kilometres as private detour kilometres (privé-omrijkilometers). (source, Dutch)

The 500 km private limit

The limit is 500 km of private driving per calendar year. If you only have the car for part of the year, you convert, and business owners and employees each do that in their own way:

  • Business owner: you scale your private kilometres up to a full year. If you drove 100 km privately in 5 months, that is 12/5 × 100 = 240 km on an annual basis. (bijzondere situaties, Dutch)
  • Employee: your limit goes down in proportion. If you have the car for 3 months, you may drive 3/12 × 500 = 125 km privately in that time. (500 privékilometers, Dutch)

Drive more than 500 km privately after all? As a business owner, you then set the bijtelling against your car costs. For a car first registered in 2026 the bijtelling is 22% of the catalogue value (cataloguswaarde). For a car without CO2 emissions it is 18% up to a catalogue value of € 30,000 (for hydrogen cars or cars powered by solar cells: 18% of the whole value). An older car keeps the percentage of the year it was first registered for 60 months. (bijtelling 2026, Dutch) As an employee, you withdraw your declaration straight away; your employer then starts adding the bijtelling to your salary. For the months before that, you can receive an additional assessment (naheffingsaanslag). (source, Dutch)

When is a trip log sluitend?

You will often read that a trip log must be sluitend: complete and consistent, with no gaps. The Belastingdienst gives no separate definition on its website, but it does say two things:

  • If you use a system with the Keurmerk RitRegistratieSystemen (a quality mark for trip logging systems), the Belastingdienst assumes your log is sluitend. It can still check whether a trip was business or private. (keurmerk, Dutch)
  • According to the Handboek Loonheffingen, a log is fully sluitend when it contains all the details listed above. It can be checked against, among other things, diaries, order slips, garage bills and route planners. (Handboek Loonheffingen 2026, paragraph 23.3.18, Dutch)

What we take from that in practice:

  • Make every start reading match the end reading of the trip before it. A gap raises questions.
  • Log a trip straight away, not from memory at the end of the month.
  • Keep whatever supports your log, such as fuel receipts and garage bills that show an odometer reading.

The Verklaring geen privégebruik auto

This declaration of no private use of a car is for employees, including a dga employed by their own bv. You apply for it yourself with the online form in Mijn Belastingdienst and give a copy to your employer. As a business owner, or if you have income from other work (resultaatgenieter), you cannot apply for it. (applying for the declaration, Dutch, business owners, Dutch)

How long do you keep a trip log?

The Belastingdienst does not mention the trip log separately. The basic records of your administration must be kept for 7 years. Other records are also kept for 7 years, unless you agree otherwise with the Belastingdienst. (bewaartermijnen, Dutch) To be safe, keep it for 7 years. If you use an app or service, download your log every year and keep it yourself, also if you stop using the service.

Questions

Can I keep my trip log in Excel?

The Belastingdienst's pages do not prescribe a form, only the details it must contain. See our free Excel template (Dutch page), which has those columns.

Does the drive from home to work count as private?

No, commuting counts as business, for business owners and for employees (see the sources above).

I changed cars this year. Does the count start again?

No. If you drive different cars one after the other in the same year, you add up the private kilometres. (business owner, Dutch, employee, Dutch) With a new employer, the kilometres you drove in your previous employer's car do not count.

I am a zzp'er driving my own car. Do I need a trip log?

Not for bijtelling, because that only applies to a car that belongs to the business. For the deduction of € 0.25 per business kilometre it is still wise to keep track of your business trips, so you can show how many kilometres you drove for business.
